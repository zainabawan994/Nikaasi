# Google Stitch prompts (Checkpoint 2)

Paste these into [Google Stitch](https://stitch.withgoogle.com) to produce the UI/UX designs. The built screens in `public/` already follow them, so the Stitch output and the working app should match.

## Shared design system (paste first)
> Design system for "Nikaasi", a flood-response app for Islamabad. Tagline "Pani se pehle, plan." Civic-works style: survey-paper background #F2EEE6, ink #1E1D1A top bars with a road-marking-yellow #E0A526 rule, Margalla green #1F4E3D primary buttons, river blue #2F6690 for water. Severity: Normal #8A8F84, Moderate #D9A21B, High #C2571A, Critical #A4161A. Fonts: Barlow Condensed (uppercase headings, buttons, numbers), IBM Plex Sans (body), IBM Plex Mono (clocks, IDs). Square corners, 1 px rules, no shadows, no gradients, no emojis or icons. Logo: flat river-blue raindrop with a yellow outflow arrow.

## Screen 1 — Citizen reporting (mobile, 390×844)
> Mobile screen "Report flooding" for Nikaasi. Navy top bar with logo, app name and a large clock. Below it, a full-width status card with a gradient coloured by severity: "Your area: G-10 · CRITICAL", one line of advice ("Severe flooding. Stay indoors. Emergency 1122"), rain mm/h, and closed roads. Then a white card: "Where are you?" sector dropdown + "📍 GPS" button, plus a small schematic grid map of sectors you can tap to drop a pin. A "What do you see?" textarea with quick-phrase chips in Roman Urdu: "Pani bhar gaya hai", "Gaari phans gayi hai", "Road band hai", "Nala overflow ho raha hai", "Koi gir gaya hai, madad chahiye". Optional photo upload with preview, optional name, and a full-width teal "Send report" button. Below that, a confirmation card: "✅ Report received. AI understood: standing water, deep; vehicle stranded. Your report joined incident NKS-0001 with 3 other reports." At the bottom, an "Alerts for your area" inbox with bilingual (English + Roman Urdu) warning cards that have an orange left border, and all-clear cards with a green border.

## Screen 2 — Authority command dashboard (desktop, 1440×1000)
> Desktop dashboard "Nikaasi Command" for municipal and traffic authorities. Navy top bar: logo, big simulated clock, "live" pulse badge, AI engine badge ("Gemini 3.5 Flash + rules-v1"), scenario buttons (Play, Pause, +1 min, speed 1×/2×/4×, Reset) and an "Auto-execute plans" toggle. Row of 6 KPI cards: Active incidents, Highest severity (badge), Citizen reports (30 min), Residents alerted, Avg trip time (with red delta vs normal), Vehicles into floodwater/h. Main area, two columns. Left (wide): a "Live zone map" card with a schematic 4×3 grid of Islamabad sectors (F-11…H-8), tinted by severity. Roads are coloured green/yellow/orange/red/dark-red by congestion, closed roads are dashed grey with a red ✕, there's an UNDERPASS tag, citizen report pins, depot icons, moving truck/ambulance icons and a blue water-depth fill. Below it, "Response impact: before vs after" with three mini-maps side by side ("At decision", "Now: with Nikaasi response", "Now: if nobody had acted") and a metrics table with green improvements. Right column: "Incidents" list (severity badge, ID, sector, status pill "plan awaiting approval"). Below it, the incident detail: a big severity score (100) with badge and "High confidence · 97%", and a stacked bar of score contributions (citizens / weather / traffic / corroboration / hazard). Then a teal-bordered explanation box ("High confidence: 5 citizen reports + active red rainfall alert + traffic spike 9.7× in G-10 within the last 20 minutes"), three signal cards (Citizens, Weather, Traffic with sparkline), and the citizen report list with the AI's reading of each. Then "Response plan v2 · CRITICAL" with action rows (🚦 Close 4 flooded segments & reroute, 🚛 Dispatch CDA Drain Team 3 with pump, 🚑 Dispatch Rescue 1122, 📲 Alert 6,755 residents), predicted impact tiles, and a large red "Approve & execute plan" button. Then dispatch tickets with 5-step progress bars. Bottom row of three cards: "Reasoning trail" vertical timeline, "Signal feeds" table per sector, "Activity log".
